Scoreboard

How enharmonic's spellers compare with published pitch spellers, dataset by dataset. Lab results, snapshot of 12 November 2026; the numbers are still moving.

Every speller gets the same MIDI notes, without key signatures, and every note is scored by the same three-tier scorer:

Exact
spelled as the composer wrote it.
Flipped
part of a passage spelled consistently on the other enharmonic side (D♭ major read as C♯ major). Coherent, so not an error.
Wrong
breaks the spelling of the notes around it (C♯–F–G♯ for C♯–E♯–G♯). The bars below rank by this.

Our four spellers

A ladder: each rung adds one idea to the one before.

  • Core: seven letter slots and interval scoring that penalises augmented and diminished intervals. Real time, about a hundred lines.
  • Real-time: adds a diatonic frame that holds the enharmonic side of a passage. Zero latency, for live MIDI.
  • Look-ahead: also reads where the next notes resolve, up to 16 notes ahead. Near real time.
  • Two-pass: a forward and a backward pass over the whole piece, then a merge. Offline.

Read with care

  • The other spellers' rows are our own runs of the implementations we could obtain or rebuild (music21, partitura's ps13, PKSpell, MuseScore, and reimplementations of the rest), scored with our scorer. They are not the authors' published figures.
  • Meredith's corpus (216 movements, clean and with human-performance timing) is the standard held-out benchmark. The lab corpus is our own curated set, so treat it as development data.
  • The stitched WTC datasets play each book as one unbroken stream. They test how a speller recovers from key changes, not its accuracy on a single piece; the per-movement datasets are the control.
  • The DCML corpora (Beethoven, Mozart, Chopin, Liszt and others, from the Digital and Cognitive Musicology Lab) are expert-encoded scores we never tuned on. Their spellings come from the encoded score. Where other spellers have not been run yet, the dataset shows ours alone.
  • PKSpell was trained on part of the lab corpus, on WTC and on ASAP (which shares pieces with several DCML corpora), so its rows there are not a fair comparison.
