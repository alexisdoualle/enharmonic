enharmonic

Real-time enharmonic pitch spelling for MIDI.

A written score names each note with a letter and an accidental. A MIDI stream keeps only the twelve pitch classes and throws that away: it cannot say whether note 61 is a C♯ or a D♭. The letter is not cosmetic. It tells a reader the note's harmonic identity. This library recovers it from musical context alone.

The idea

A spelling can be wrong in two independent ways. The first is coherence: inside an F‑minor chord, note 68 is A♭, not G♯, because G♯ would make an augmented second where the ear hears a minor third. The second is the comma, or side: a passage in C♯ major written in D♭ major is the same pitches one turn around the spiral of fifths, a coherent transposition, not an error.

The speller works in one harmonic space, with no key detection anywhere. Three principles handle coherence: a running seven-letter scale, an interval score that rewards consonance, and a recency guard against flicker. A spiral of fifths then decides which side of the comma a passage sits on. The side it lands on is a statistic of the notes it has already committed.

Those three principles are small enough to read in one file: the Core speller, about 100 lines with zero imports. On its own it spells coherently but can land on the wrong side of the comma, so its coherent rate stays high while its exact rate drops. The shipped modes add the side correction that closes that gap.

One speller

One engine spells each note as it plays, from past context only. An optional look-ahead setting reads a small forward buffer to fix the direction a semitone resolves. An offline two-pass option reads the whole piece forward and backward, then reconciles the two to repair the modulation boundaries the streaming pass spells late.

Exact is a composer-spelling match. On Meredith's held-out corpus (195,972 notes), the standard pitch-spelling benchmark, the library is level with the best deterministic and neural spellers. The 59-piece curated corpus is harder and less overfit: it stresses the side (comma) choice Meredith mostly avoids. There the exact rate falls (steeply for Core), but coherent stays high. Coherent counts a whole-passage flip to the other side of the spiral as correct, since it is the same music transposed, not a wrong note. Almost every miss is a flip, not an error.
